A neighborhood message is not an emergency report.

Emergency or immediate danger

911

For fire, medical emergencies, a crime in progress, or an immediate threat. Durham also directs reports of suspicious activity happening now to 911. If you are unsure whether it is an emergency, call 911.

Durham’s 911 guidance ↗

Police · non-emergency

919-560-4600

For police assistance when there is no immediate danger, such as a past incident. Explain what happened so the dispatcher can decide the appropriate response.

Police contact information ↗

Notice behavior. Share facts.

An unfamiliar person or vehicle alone is not evidence of wrongdoing. Describe what you actually saw, the location, time, and direction of travel. Do not confront, follow, or investigate someone. Stay somewhere safe and follow the dispatcher’s instructions.

After reporting, share relevant information privately with your block captain. Avoid public accusations, speculation, and identifying photos of uninvolved neighbors.

Home safety

Small habits,
every day.

Choose a topic for practical steps.
Start with what matters most for your home.

Doors, windows & garagesMake locking up an everyday habit.+
  • Lock exterior doors and accessible windows, including the door between your garage and home. Close the garage when you are not using it.
  • Use your alarm if you have one. If you return to signs of a break-in, stay outside, move somewhere safe, and call 911.
Lighting & visibilityHelp people see entrances and paths.+
  • Keep entryways well lit and trim landscaping that obscures doors or windows. Consider motion-activated lighting aimed at your property to reduce glare into neighboring homes.
  • Keep valuables out of sight from windows. Check that your house number is easy for responders to find.
Cars, deliveries & valuablesLeave less on display.+
  • Lock your vehicle and remove valuables, keys, and garage remotes. Arrange prompt pickup of deliveries when possible.
  • Keep a private inventory of valuable items with photographs and serial numbers. Store it securely; do not post it in neighborhood groups.
While you’re awayPlan with someone you trust.+
  • Use light timers and arrange mail or package collection. Ask a trusted neighbor to check on your home.
  • Share travel plans privately with people who need them. Save public vacation posts for after your return.
Cameras & connected devicesProtect your accounts as well as your home.+
  • Replace default passwords with unique passwords, enable two-factor authentication where offered, and keep camera, router, and app software updated.
  • Review who can access recordings. Aim cameras thoughtfully and share relevant evidence privately with investigators rather than publishing accusations.
Unexpected visitors & messagesTake a moment to verify.+
  • Verify an unexpected worker or payment request using an organization’s independently obtained contact information. You do not need to open your door to speak to a visitor.
  • Do not give out passwords, verification codes, or account details in response to an unexpected message. Contact CAS through the known community link for HOA payment questions.

Further reading: Durham Police home safety guide (PDF), FTC camera security, and FTC account and scam prevention advice. These suggestions reduce opportunities for problems; they cannot guarantee safety.
